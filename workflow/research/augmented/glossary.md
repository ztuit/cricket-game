# Glossary — Cricket Game
**Status:** published
**Date:** 2026-08-20

All terms below are defined as used in the opportunity document and human responses. Terms are standard cricket vocabulary unless noted otherwise.

---

## Cricket domain terms

| Term | Definition as used in context | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Batsman** | The player receiving the delivery. In this game, the human player. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 16. Standard cricket term. |
| **Batter** | Modern gender-neutral term for batsman. Used interchangeably. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). The game uses "batsman" but "batter" is acceptable. |
| **Bowler** | The entity bowling the ball at the batsman. In this game, an AI-controlled fictional character with specific characteristics and personality. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 18 (note: line 18 says "batsmen" — this is a typo, should be "bowlers"). |
| **Ball** | A single delivery bowled from the bowler to the batsman. One unit of gameplay. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, lines 12–14. |
| **Delivery** | Synonym for ball — a single act of bowling. | Used interchangeably with "ball" in cricket. |
| **Over** | A set of six consecutive deliveries bowled by the same bowler from one end. | Source: human response to OQ-01. In T20, each bowler can bowl a maximum of 4 overs. |
| **Innings** | A period of play where one team bats. In this game, the player's innings is the match. | Implied by T20 format. |
| **Line** | The horizontal position of the ball's trajectory relative to the stumps (e.g., outside off stump, on middle, leg side). | Source: `cricket-game.md`, lines 6, 12. |
| **Length** | Where the ball pitches on the surface relative to the batsman (e.g., full/toss, good length, short). | Source: `cricket-game.md`, lines 6, 12, 16. |
| **Pace** | The speed at which the ball is bowled. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 12. |
| **Spin** | Rotational movement of the ball, typically imparted by a spin bowler. Limited information is shown to the player to increase difficulty. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 12. |
| **Field / field arrangement / field placement** | The positions of the fielders on the ground. Presented to the player before the delivery. Selected from predefined sets based on bowler type and match conditions. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, lines 6, 10, 23; human response to OQ-11. |
| **Fielders** | The non-bowling members of the bowling team positioned on the ground. Their placement affects outcome (e.g., caught in the slips). | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 16. |
| **Slips** | A group of fielders positioned behind the batsman on the off side, primarily to catch edges. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 16. |
| **Shot** | The batsman's action of hitting the ball. The primary player input in this game. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, lines 6, 14, 16. |
| **Drive** | A vertical-bat shot played by swinging the bat in a vertical arc. Variants: straight drive, off drive, on drive, cover drive, square drive. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Cover drive** | A drive struck towards the cover fielding position. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Pull** | A horizontal-bat shot played to a waist-high delivery, swinging the bat horizontally to the leg side. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Hook** | Like a pull, but played to a chest-high or above delivery. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Cut** | A horizontal-bat shot played to a short-pitched ball, placing it wide on the off side. Variants: square cut, late cut. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Sweep** | A horizontal-bat front foot shot played to a low-bouncing ball, usually from a spin bowler. Variants: paddle sweep, hard sweep. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Defensive shot / block** | A shot designed to stop the ball from hitting the wicket or the batter's body. No strength behind it. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Leave** | The batsman deliberately not playing at the ball. Must ensure bat/hands are out of the path to avoid accidental contact. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Leg glance / flick** | A delicate straight-batted shot played at a ball on the leg side, using the bat to flick the ball as it passes. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Reverse sweep** | A cross-batted sweep played in the opposite direction to the standard sweep (to the off side). High risk. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Slog** | A powerful pull shot played over mid-wicket, usually in the air for a six. Higher risk than a standard pull. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Upper cut** | A shot played towards third man, usually to a short-pitched ball outside off stump with extra bounce. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Switch hit** | The batter changes handedness and stance while the bowler is running in, wrong-footing the fielders. Very high risk. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Scoop / ramp** | The batter gets beneath the bounce and hits the ball over the wicket-keeper. Very high risk. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Helicopter shot** | A wristy flick using the bottom hand, with the bat circling overhead. Effective against yorkers. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Wicket** | In context: the batsman being dismissed (out). "Result in a wicket" means the batsman is out. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 16. Can also refer to the physical stumps. |
| **Caught** | A dismissal where a fielder catches the ball after the batsman has hit it. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 16: "caught in the slips." |
| **Bowled** | A dismissal where the ball hits the stumps directly. | Implied by `cricket-game.md`, line 16. |
| **LBW (Leg Before Wicket)** | A dismissal where the ball would have hit the stumps but was intercepted by the batsman's body. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). Not mentioned in opportunity doc but a standard dismissal type. |
| **Run** | The unit of scoring in cricket. Scored by the batsmen running between the wickets or hitting the ball to the boundary. | Implied by T20 format. |
| **Boundary** | The edge of the playing area. A ball reaching the boundary along the ground scores 4 runs; in the air, 6 runs. | Standard cricket term. |
| **Six** | A hit that clears the boundary on the fly. 6 runs. | Standard cricket term. |
| **Four** | A hit that reaches the boundary along the ground. 4 runs. | Standard cricket term. |
| **Wide** | An illegal delivery that is too far from the batsman to hit. The batting team gets 1 run and the ball is re-bowled. | Human response to OQ-09 mentions "% of wide/no balls." |
| **No ball** | An illegal delivery (e.g., bowler oversteps the crease). The batting team gets 1 run and the ball is re-bowled. | Human response to OQ-09. |
| **Full** (delivery) | A ball that pitches close to the batsman, making it easier to drive. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 16. |
| **Good length** | A ball that pitches at a distance where the batsman is unsure whether to play forward or back. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 16. |
| **Short** (delivery) | A ball that pitches further from the batsman, bouncing higher. Typically played off the back foot. | Standard cricket term. |
| **Yorker** | A full-length delivery that pitches at or near the batsman's feet. Very difficult to score off. | Wikipedia: Batting (cricket). |
| **Spinner** | A bowler who primarily uses spin rather than pace. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 16. |
| **Ground** | The cricket venue/pitch where the match is played. Each ground has weather conditions. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 18. |
| **Pitch** | The prepared strip of ground where the ball bounces. Also used synonymously with "ground" in some contexts. | Source: human response to OQ-15: "vary across the pitch." |
| **Stumps** | The three vertical poles that form the wicket. The bowler aims to hit them; the batsman defends them. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 16. |
| **Crease** | The line the batsman stands behind. Also the line the bowler must not overstep. | Standard cricket term. |
| **Toss** | The coin toss at the start of the match that determines who bats first. | Source: human response to OQ-01. |
| **Target** | The score the batting team must reach to win when chasing. | Source: human response to OQ-01. |
| **T20** | Twenty20 cricket — a format with a maximum of 20 overs per innings. | Source: human response to OQ-01. |

## Game-specific terms

These are terms used in the opportunity document or human responses that describe game mechanics rather than standard cricket vocabulary.

| Term | Definition as used in context | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Coefficient of the surface** | A variable representing the condition of the pitch/surface at a specific point. Changes over time, varies across the pitch, affected by weather, ball deliveries, and ball age. Influences how the ball bounces. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 13; human response to OQ-15. Not a standard cricket term — a game mechanic abstraction. |
| **Wrist angle** | A player-controlled input representing the angle of the bat/wrist at the point of contact. Selected via a visual dial showing degree rotation. Affects where the ball goes. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 14; human response to OQ-10. |
| **Card** | A UI element showing all details of a player (batsman or bowler). Can be opened at any time during the game. | Source: human response to OQ-14. |
| **Stats of the bowler** | Statistical information about the bowler shown to the player before the delivery. Full details available on the bowler's card. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 10; human response to OQ-14. |
| **Limited time** | The time constraint within which the player must choose their shot. | Source: `cricket-game.md`, line 14. Exact duration not specified. |
| **Experience class** | A classification of a bowler's skill level. Higher experience class = better accuracy, success rate, fewer wides/no-balls. | Source: human response to OQ-09. |
| **Bowler type** | The category of bowler (e.g., fast, medium, off-spin, leg-spin). Defines the type of bowling delivered. | Source: human response to OQ-09. Specific types TBD from research. |
| **Field set** | A predefined arrangement of fielders. Selected based on bowler type and match conditions. | Source: human response to OQ-11. |
| **Character / quirk** | Non-cricket personality traits of fictional players that add entertainment value. Includes nicknames. | Source: human response to OQ-13. |

## Inconsistent or ambiguous usage

| Issue | Detail |
|---|---|
| **"Balls will be bowled by batsmen"** (line 18) | Typo. Should be "bowlers." Downstream agents should treat this as "bowlers." |
| **"Line and length"** | Used as a compound phrase (line 6) and separately (line 12). In cricket, these are distinct concepts that together describe a delivery's trajectory. The game treats them as separate inputs to the outcome model. |
| **"Shot type" vs "shot"** | "Shot type" (line 14) implies a category (e.g., drive, pull, cut), while "shot" alone could mean the specific action. The document does not enumerate shot types — this glossary now provides the standard set. |
| **"Visual" vs "text"** | Line 23–24 suggests two presentation modes. Human clarified: visual is mandatory, text is not pursued. "Visual" style is not yet decided (2D top-down vs comic book). |
| **"Pitch" ambiguity** | "Pitch" can mean the playing surface (where the ball bounces) or the ground/venue. In this game, "pitch" primarily means the surface, and "ground" means the venue. |
