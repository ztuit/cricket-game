# Product Vision — Cricket Game v1
**Status:** draft
**Date:** 2026-08-20
**Version:** v1

---

## The opportunity

Cricket is the world's second-most-watched sport, with an estimated 2.5 billion fans globally. T20 cricket — the fast, explosive format — has driven massive growth, especially in the UK, India, Australia, and the Caribbean. Yet there is no mobile game that genuinely tests a cricket fan's tactical knowledge. Existing cricket games test reflexes (timing-based batting) or management skills (team simulators). None ask the question a real batsman faces: *given this delivery, this field, and these conditions, what shot should I play?*

That gap is the opportunity. A game where cricket knowledge is the skill — where knowing that a backfoot shot to a full ball on stumps is suicide, or that slips are telling you the bowler wants an edge — is the difference between hitting a six and getting bowled.

---

## The product

**Cricket Game** is a character-driven cricket strategy game for mobile phones (Android 12+).

The player bats through a T20 match — 20 overs, 10 wickets, real teams and grounds — facing deliveries from AI bowlers who are fictional characters with nicknames, quirks, and personality. Think comic book cricketers: bowlers with names like "The Magician" or "Golden Arm," each with a distinct bowling style and a character card you can open at any time.

**The core loop, per delivery:**
1. Read the field — what are the fielders telling you about the bowler's plan?
2. See the ball — line, length, pace, and *limited* spin information (you don't get to see everything a real batsman wouldn't know)
3. Choose your shot — pick a shot type and dial in your wrist angle, under time pressure
4. Watch it play out — a probability model, weighted by your decision, the surface conditions, and the bowler's skill, determines whether you score or get out

**What makes it more than a guessing game:**
- The pitch surface is alive. It varies across the pitch, degrades over the match, reacts to weather, and changes based on how many balls have been bowled to each area. A dry pitch on day five plays differently from a fresh green one.
- Difficulty scales in two layers: within a match (the bowler adapts, coefficients shift as the innings progresses) and across your career (tougher opponents as your team advances).
- Spin is deliberately obscured. You get *some* information about the spin on each delivery, but not all — just like a real batsman reading the ball out of the bowler's hand. This is the core tension: making the best decision with incomplete information, under time pressure.

## The benefit

**For the cricket fan:** A game that respects their knowledge. Every delivery is a small tactical puzzle that rewards understanding of cricket — field placements, shot selection, pitch conditions. Winning feels earned, not lucky.

**For the casual player (via recommendation):** The character system — nicknames, quirks, personality cards — adds entertainment value even if you don't fully understand the cricket nuance. The game is fun to watch someone else play, and the characters give you something to root for.

**For the creator:** A clear, focused product with a strong core loop. The T20 format keeps sessions short (~20 minutes for a full match, 1 over for the free tier). The offline-first architecture means MVP 1 ships without server complexity, while the domain model is designed to support leaderboards, accounts, and multiplayer later.

## Features

**Core (must be in every version):**
- T20 match format: 20 overs, 10 wickets, toss mechanic
- Delivery-by-delivery batting with shot selection and wrist angle
- Surface physics model (pitch variation, weather, ball age, degradation)
- AI bowlers with type, experience class, and personality
- Field placement system (predefined sets, adapted to conditions)
- Player cards (batsmen and bowlers — stats, nickname, quirks)
- Match-level difficulty progression (coefficients shift within a match)
- Real teams and grounds

**Character layer (differentiator):**
- Fictional players with nicknames, cricket skills, and non-cricket quirks
- Comic-book-inspired personality presentation
- Character cards accessible at any time

**Meta-game (career layer):**
- Team progression system (details TBD — the player advances their team and faces tougher opponents)
- Career-level difficulty scaling

**Monetisation:**
- Freemium model: free tier (taste of the game), paid tier (full match experience)
- Exact lock mechanics TBD — must feel generous, not predatory

**Future (architect for, don't build yet):**
- Online leaderboards
- Player accounts and cloud saves
- Multiplayer
- Community features

## What success looks like

**For the player:** "I lost that match because I played the wrong shot to a spinner on a wearing pitch — and I *knew* it was the wrong choice the moment I committed. I want to try again."

**For the creator:** A playable T20 match on Android that cricket fans enjoy, with a clear path to monetisation and online features. The core loop works — people come back for another match.

**For the market:** A cricket game that fills a gap nobody else is filling. Not a reflex game, not a manager — a *strategy* game that respects the sport's tactical depth.

## Risks and issues

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visual style doesn't resonate — cheap or dated look | Medium | High | Prototype early. Test with real users before committing to a rendering approach. The "character-driven" nature suggests comic book style has the highest ceiling. |
| Shot selection UI is overwhelming under time pressure | Medium | High | Curate to 8–12 shots. Group logically. UX testing with real cricket fans is essential. |
| Surface physics model is too complex to feel fair to the player | Medium | Medium | Player must be able to *see* or *infer* the relevant conditions. Hidden complexity that punishes the player without explanation is frustration, not depth. |
| Freemium model doesn't convert — 1 over isn't enough to hook | Medium | High | The free tier must deliver a complete emotional arc (tension, decision, outcome). Iterate on what's free based on conversion data. |
| Real team/ground names require licensing | Low | Medium | Legal review before launch. Fallback: use fictional but recognisable team names. |
| Cricket knowledge barrier excludes casual players | Medium | Medium | Character system and UI design can bridge the gap. Not the primary audience, but shouldn't be locked out entirely. |
| Solo developer capacity — scope creep kills the project | High | Critical | Ruthless MVP scoping. Ship the core loop first. Everything else is negotiable. |

---

## Out of scope

- **Multiplayer** — architect for it, don't build it in v1
- **Online accounts / cloud saves** — future feature
- **Bowling** — the player always bats in this version
- **Detailed AI batting simulation** — when setting a target, the AI score is generated, not simulated ball-by-ball
- **Tutorial / onboarding system** — MVP 1 assumes the player knows cricket. A gentle onboarding experience is a fast follow.
- **Tablet-optimised layouts** — phones first, tablets later
- **iOS** — Android first. iOS is a future platform decision.
